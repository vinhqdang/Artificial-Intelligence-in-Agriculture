TITLE
Differentiable simulation for agrivoltaic tracker control under a yield floor: what learning adds and what shade-response uncertainty costs

ABSTRACT (241 words)
Agrivoltaic single-axis trackers can rotate to let light through, but how much a learned controller adds over simple rules under a yield-retention floor, and how shade-response uncertainty limits design, are unknown. We built a differentiable simulator of tracker geometry, photovoltaic output, light and microclimate under the modules, and crop growth, and trained a neural controller through whole seasons by back-propagation. The controller and written rules were compared on 400 crop–site pairs, with each site's density chosen from its history and evaluation on held-out sites. Light-sharing rules allowed arrays 2.1–2.9 times as dense as static arrays and raised the land-equivalent ratio (LER) from 1.07 to 1.18–1.24, but the floor was missed in 24–31% of seasons. The neural controller exceeded the written phenology rule by 0.044 LER and an optimised window by only 0.007, with no detectable difference at matched retention. Shade curves learned from 428 field observations were no better than proportional loss for staple crops, and the simulator was milder than the data for maize. A density chosen under the mean response left 70% of pairs at the floor when another plausible response held (oracle: 87%). Conformal margins attained the stated compliance within an ensemble of responses, a learned design network did worse than a simple margin, and only large margins were safe outside the ensemble. Differentiable simulation made control and design tractable, but data, not the learner, limit what can be guaranteed. The evaluation is by simulation on public data.

KEYWORDS
Agrivoltaics; Differentiable simulation; Solar tracking; Neural control; Crop modelling; Conformal prediction; Decision under uncertainty

HIGHLIGHTS
- Differentiable simulator trains agrivoltaic tracker control by back-propagation.
- A neural controller adds 0.007 LER over a tuned window, null at equal retention.
- Conformal margins keep compliance in-ensemble; a learned design network is worse.
- Shade-response uncertainty, not the learner, limits yield-floor compliance.
- Rule-based light sharing allows 2-3x denser trackers at a 90% yield floor.
