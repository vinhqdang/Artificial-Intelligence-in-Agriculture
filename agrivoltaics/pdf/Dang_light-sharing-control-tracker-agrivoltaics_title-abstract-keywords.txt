TITLE
Light-sharing control for tracker-based agrivoltaics under a yield floor: what learning adds and what shade-response uncertainty costs

ABSTRACT (structured, 238 words)
Context: Agrivoltaic trackers can rotate to let light through (light sharing); its value under a yield floor, and what learning adds, are unclear. Objective: To quantify light sharing under a 90% retention floor, test what learning adds and measure the cost of shade-response uncertainty. Methods: A differentiable simulator of tracker, PV, light and crop growth was applied to 400 crop–site pairs, with densities chosen from each site's history and evaluation on held-out sites. Results: Light-sharing rules allowed arrays 2.1–2.9 times as dense as static arrays and raised the land-equivalent ratio (LER) from 1.07 to 1.18–1.24, but the floor was missed in 24–31% of seasons. A neural controller exceeded the written phenology rule by 0.044 LER but an optimised window by only 0.007, and by 0.003 at matched retention (no detectable difference). Learned shade curves were no better than proportional loss for staple crops (error 0.22), and the simulator was milder than the data for maize. A density chosen under the mean response left 70% of pairs at the floor when another plausible response held (oracle: 87%); conformal margins attained the stated compliance inside the ensemble, a learned design did worse than a simple margin, and only large margins were safe outside it. Light sharing breaks even with the open field at 70–92 USD/MWh. Conclusions: Light sharing by rule is the main lever for single-axis trackers; uncertainty about the crop response, not the learner, limits what can be guaranteed.

KEYWORDS
Agrivoltaics; Light sharing; Solar tracking; Crop modelling; Differentiable simulation; Decision under uncertainty; Food–energy nexus
