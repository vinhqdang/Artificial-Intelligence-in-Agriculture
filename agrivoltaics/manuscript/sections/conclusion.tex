\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusion}
A differentiable simulator of trackers, PV output, crop light, microclimate and crop growth allowed us to compare agrivoltaic controllers fairly on 400 crop--site pairs, with the array density of each site chosen from its own history. At a mean yield retention of 90\%, light-sharing control allows about three times the module density of static arrays, raising the land-equivalent ratio from 1.07 to 1.18--1.24 with hand-written rules and to 1.27 with a learned controller. The learned controller improves on the best rule by 0.03--0.05 in LER (about 10\% more electricity), reproducibly over seeds, spatial splits, canopy-cooling assumptions and climates. The improvement comes from a smooth phenology-dependent schedule, not from reacting to heat or drought. The yield floor is met on average but not in every season or region, and the electricity potential of 5\% of staple cropland (about 14\,PWh\,yr$^{-1}$) requires about 10\,TWp of modules. Future work should validate the crop response to shade under trackers with field data, extend the crop model to grain-number formation and canopy energy balance, add costs and grid constraints, and pursue controllers that guarantee the food-security floor per site and per season.
