\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}
Light sharing, the daily choice between maximum electricity and letting light through to the crop, is the main lever (by rule, not by learning) on the electricity of tracker-based agrivoltaics at a given yield-retention floor. Evaluated on held-out crop--site pairs, with the density of each site chosen from its own history, light-sharing rules allowed 2.1 to 2.9 times the module density of static arrays at a mean retention of 90\% and raised the land-equivalent ratio from 1.07 to 1.18--1.24. A neural controller trained through a differentiable simulator exceeded the written phenology rule by 0.044 LER but a window optimised by CMA-ES by only 0.007, and by an amount not distinguishable from zero at matched retention; ensemble training and a biomass signal did not help.

The crop response to shade is the least certain input. A model fitted to 428 observations predicts a new study to about 0.22 in relative yield, no better than proportional loss for the staple crop types, and the simulator is milder than the data for maize. A density chosen under the mean response left about 70\% of pairs at the floor when another plausible response held, against 87\% for an oracle design. Conformal calibration attains a stated compliance inside the ensemble and a learned design network does no better than a simple margin; no rule was safe outside the ensemble.

With illustrative costs, light sharing breaks even with the open field at 70 to 92\,USD\,MWh$^{-1}$ at floor-designed densities (73 for SALS, 59 for static arrays), the difference between SALS and static arrays at 90\,USD\,MWh$^{-1}$ is not distinguishable from zero, and a conventional plant replacing the crop is more profitable above 48 (about 64 at equal capex). The electricity potential of 5\% of staple cropland (about 14\,PWh\,yr$^{-1}$, a scaling convention without an interval) requires about 10\,TWp of modules. For agricultural decision-making, the practical message is to treat the crop response as a distribution, to calibrate the design margin per crop instead of choosing it by hand, and to expect that no margin protects against responses outside the range considered.
