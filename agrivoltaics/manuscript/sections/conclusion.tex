\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusion}
We have presented SALS, a learning algorithm that designs and controls agrivoltaic systems end-to-end through a differentiable simulator of trackers, PV output, crop light, microclimate, soil water and crop growth. Trained on 400 cropland sites of the world's five major staple crops and four warming levels, SALS produced 72--85\% of the electricity of a conventional solar plant while keeping the mean crop yield at 90\% of the open field. This raised the land-equivalent ratio from 1.2 for static designs and 1.4 for expert light-sharing rules to 1.6--1.75 on geographically held-out sites and years. The learned policy shares light during canopy development and shades the crop during heat, and its advantage grows with warming. Scaled to 5\% of the harvested area of the five crops, learned agrivoltaics would have a technical potential comparable to current global electricity generation. Future work should add chance constraints for year-by-year food security, crop-specific shade physiology and field validation of the learned control on commercial tracker systems.
