\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Context:} Agrivoltaic trackers can rotate to let light through to the crop (light sharing); its value under a yield floor, and what learning adds, are unclear. \textbf{Objective:} To quantify light sharing under a 90\% retention floor, test what learning adds and measure the cost of shade-response uncertainty. \textbf{Methods:} A differentiable simulator of tracker, PV, light, microclimate and crop growth was applied to 400 crop--site pairs under four climates, with densities chosen from each site's history and evaluation on held-out sites and years. \textbf{Results:} Light-sharing rules allowed arrays 2.1--2.9 times as dense as static arrays and raised the land-equivalent ratio (LER) from 1.07 to 1.18--1.24. A neural controller exceeded the written phenology rule by 0.044 LER but an optimised window by only 0.007 (95\% interval 0.002--0.014), and by 0.003 at matched retention (not distinguishable from zero). A model fitted to 428 observations predicted a new study no better than proportional loss (error 0.22). A density chosen under the mean response left about 70\% of pairs at the floor when another plausible response held, against 87\% for an oracle; conformal margins attained a stated compliance inside the ensemble, a learned design network did no better than a simple margin, and neither was safe outside it. With illustrative costs, light sharing breaks even with the open field at 70--92\,USD\,MWh$^{-1}$. \textbf{Conclusions:} Light sharing by rule is the main lever; the uncertainty of the crop response, not the learner, limits what can be guaranteed.
\end{abstract}
